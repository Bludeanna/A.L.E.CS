    Concepts & Formulas

    Projectile motion happens when an object is launched and moves under gravity alone. Let's break it down.

    🎯 What is Projectile Motion?

    A projectile is any object thrown or launched into the air that moves only under the influence of gravity. The path it follows is called a parabola. The horizontal and vertical components of motion are independent of each other — horizontal speed stays constant, while gravity constantly pulls the object downward.

    Everyday examples include shooting a basketball 🏀, kicking a football ⚽, or throwing a javelin.

    📐 Key Formulas
    x = v₀cosθ · t

    Horizontal distance at time t. Constant velocity — no horizontal acceleration.

    y = v₀sinθ · t − ½gt²

    Vertical position at time t. Gravity (g = 10 m/s²) pulls it downward.

    R = v₀²·sin2θ / g

    Range — total horizontal distance. Maximized at 45°.

    H = v₀²·sin²θ / 2g

    Max Height — highest point reached. Maximized at 90°.

    T = 2v₀sinθ / g

    Time of Flight — how long it stays airborne. Doubles if you double v₀.

    🧠 Concept Reinforcement

    At 45°, the range is maximized — sin(2×45°) = sin(90°) = 1, so R = v₀²/g.
    Increasing velocity increases both range AND height.
    Too high an angle (>70°) gives great height but poor range — the ball goes more up than forward.
    Too low an angle (<20°) gives poor height — the ball hits the ground before reaching the hoop.
